The Drowned Coast

Starting region 31 pages Levels 1–6

A drowned valley system on the western littoral, four working ports, and the place where most of what fell came ashore. If your table is starting anywhere, start here.

The coast was a valley system before the fall and is an archipelago now. Ridgelines became headlands, farmland became shallows, and three river mouths became the deepwater channels that make the ports possible. Nobody planned any of it, which is why every settlement here is built on the wrong side of its own harbour.

What matters at the table is simple: this is where essence enters the economy. Everything else about the region — the toll, the cartel, the Synod's assay offices, the fact that four ports cannot agree on a tide table — follows from that one fact.

Geography

Roughly four hundred miles of broken coast, sheltered along most of its length by a chain of headlands that used to be a ridge. The water inside them is deep, cold and unusually clear; outside them it is none of those things. Travel is by sea because the interior roads flooded and were never rebuilt.

The seabed does not hold still. Surveyors date charts by season rather than year, and a chart three seasons old is sold as a curiosity rather than a tool.

Weather and the tides

Two tides a day, neither reliable. The interval drifts across a season by as much as an hour, and the drift is not the same in every port — which is the single most consequential piece of local knowledge on the coast. Weather comes from the west in autumn and from nowhere in particular the rest of the year.

Who holds it

House Marrow holds the largest port and the toll that comes with it. House Calder holds the refineries inland and the roads to them. The Free Tide Cartel holds everything in between, in the sense that nothing moves between ports without its knowledge.

The Synod maintains assay offices in all four ports. Every one of them operates on a licence the local house reviews annually, which tells you exactly how much independence an assayer has.

Settlements

Four ports, nine villages
I have charted this coast twice. The second chart disagrees with the first in eleven places, and I stand behind both of them. Coastal passage between adjacent ports takes one day and costs 2 grains as deck passage. Crossing outside the headlands halves the time and requires a Wisdom (Survival) check at DC 15 against the weather.
