Session one

Running the salvage job

The opening adventure gives you a tide table, a wreck, and three parties who want what is in it. It is deliberately small: one port, one night, one decision that the players cannot make cleanly.

What to prep

Read the tide table and pick which house is waiting on the dock. That is the whole prep. The adventure carries the rest, including the two NPCs your players will remember and the one who will be lying to them.

What to say at the table

Open with the smell of the bay and the price of essence chalked on a board. Starfall lands when money and divinity are in the same sentence, so put them there in the first minute.

From the Ledger

Essence is weighed in grains, sold in vials, and taxed in whatever the local house can enforce. If your players ask what a grain is worth, the answer is always "here, today, this much".

Safety and tone

The quickstart includes a one-page tone sheet: what the setting does with debt, drowning and religious pressure, and how to dial each one down. Use it in session zero. It exists so nobody has to guess.

Getting started

Questions GMs actually ask

Do my players need to buy anything?
No. Players need a 5e character and the free map. Everything GM-facing is in the quickstart.
I bought the PDF — where is it?
In the email from your order, and permanently in your download library. Premium PDFs are updated in place, so the link always gives you the current edition.
Can I run this without the setting book?
Yes, for several sessions. The quickstart covers one port and the essence rules; the book is what you want once players start asking about the interior.
Is there a VTT version?
Map packs for Foundry and Roll20 ship with each adventure. The player map is a plain PNG at 100 px per hex.
How long is the first session?
Three hours for five players, including character questions. It shortens cleanly by cutting the second buyer on the dock.
